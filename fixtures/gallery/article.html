How terminal browsers render the web

By A. Writer · 8 Oct 2026 · 6 min read · Share

Rendering a web page into a grid of characters is an exercise in compromise. A cell is eight pixels wide and sixteen tall, a paragraph in a proportional font rarely lines up with it, and yet the result can be remarkably readable when the renderer respects the structure of the page rather than its pixels.

Text first

The first rule is that text should stay text. When a paragraph is drawn as coloured blocks it turns into noise; when it is drawn as characters it stays crisp and selectable, even if its position is only approximate. Links keep their underline and colour, headings keep their weight.

Structure survives quantisation; pixels do not.
